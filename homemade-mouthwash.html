Homemade Peppermint Mouthwash

A simple baking soda and salt rinse with peppermint oil. A freshening, soothing mouth rinse — not a replacement for brushing, flossing or fluoride.

Makes 1 cup (250 ml) · Prep 5 minutes · Total 5 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (250 ml) distilled or boiled-and-cooled water
  • 1 tbsp baking soda
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2–3 drops peppermint essential oil (food/oral grade)
  • 1 tsp vegetable glycerin (optional)

Method

  1. Add the baking soda and salt to the water and stir until fully dissolved.
  2. Add the peppermint oil and the glycerin, if using. The oil will not fully dissolve, which is expected.
  3. Pour into a clean glass bottle with a tight lid. Label it clearly so it is not mistaken for a drink.
  4. Shake well before each use. Swish about 2 tablespoons for 30 seconds, then spit out. Do not swallow.

Notes

Keep to a few drops of essential oil per cup. Avoid clove, cinnamon and wintergreen oils — they irritate mouth tissue at higher concentrations.

Made with distilled or boiled water and kept sealed, it holds for roughly a week. There is no preservative, so make small batches.

No fluoride and no CPC or chlorhexidine, so this freshens and soothes but does not do the antibacterial work of a commercial rinse. See a dentist before relying on it for gum disease or any ongoing dental issue.

Variation: plain saltwater rinse

1 cup warm water with 1/2 teaspoon salt. Good after dental work or for minor irritation.
