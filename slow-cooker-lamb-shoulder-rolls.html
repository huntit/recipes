Lamb Shoulder Rolls with Rosemary and Garlic

Ten hours on LOW while the house is empty, shredded Saturday evening, warmed through Sunday morning. Lunch lands at 12:30 with fifteen minutes of actual work on the day.

What you need

The pot

  • 2 kg bone-in lamb shoulder
  • 2 brown onions, thickly sliced
  • 8 garlic cloves, whole
  • 4 rosemary sprigs
  • 250 ml water or no-added-salt stock
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp black pepper, ground
  • 1½ tsp salt — added at the very end

Yoghurt-mint sauce

  • 250 g Greek yoghurt
  • 3 tbsp mint leaves, chopped
  • 1 lemon, juiced

On the table

  • 8 large sourdough rolls
  • 1 jar mint sauce, shop-bought
  • 150 g cheddar, grated
  • 1 baby cos, leaves separated
  • 2 Lebanese cucumbers, sliced
  • 3 tomatoes, sliced
  • Butter — salted and unsalted

Method

  1. Friday evening — stud the lamb

    Pat the shoulder dry and rub it all over with the olive oil and pepper. Make eight small incisions in the fat with the tip of a sharp knife and push a whole garlic clove into each one. Cover and refrigerate. Slice the onions now too and keep them in a container — that is the entire prep done.

  2. Saturday, ten to seven — load and go

    Boil the water or stock in the kettle first. Scatter the onions across the base of the cooker so they form a trivet, sit the lamb on top, tuck the rosemary around it and pour the hot liquid in. No salt yet. Lid on, LOW.

    Why boiling liquid A cold 2 kg joint takes a long time to bring a cooker up to temperature. Starting with hot liquid closes that gap, which matters when nobody is home to check. Do not fill past two-thirds of the bowl.

  3. Ten hours, unattended

    LOW is the right setting, not HIGH. High heat drives moisture out of the muscle fibres faster than the collagen converts to gelatin, and you end up with meat that is cooked through but stringy. Low and long gives you the silky, fork-twisting texture. It is ready when a fork turns the meat away from the bone with no resistance.

  4. Saturday evening — rest, shred, chill

    Lift the lamb onto a board and rest it 15 minutes. Discard the rosemary stems and the bone, then shred with two forks, leaving behind any large lumps of fat. Strain the cooking liquid into a jug and skim the fat off the top. Meat in one container, juices in another, both into the fridge.

    This is an upgrade, not a compromise Cooking a day ahead genuinely improves shoulder. It sits in its own juices overnight and reabsorbs them, so the reheated version is moister than it was straight out of the pot.

  5. Saturday evening — the yoghurt-mint sauce

    Stir the yoghurt, chopped mint and half the lemon juice together. Cover and refrigerate. Hold the rest of the juice back until tomorrow — acid draws water out of yoghurt overnight and you will get a pool of whey on top. The mint will darken a shade by morning, which is cosmetic only.

  6. Sunday, 10:30 — reheat

    Tip the shredded lamb and the strained juices back into the cooker and set it to LOW. Give it about an hour and three quarters. Stir once if you pass by, but it does not need it.

  7. Sunday, 12:15 — split before you salt

    Lift roughly a sixth of the lamb into a separate warm bowl and leave it completely unsalted. Now stir the salt through everything left in the pot, taste, and adjust. Stir the reserved lemon juice into the yoghurt sauce and set aside a few unsalted spoonfuls of that too.

    Low-sodium serve Lamb is naturally around 70 mg sodium per 100 g, so an unsalted portion sits well under 200 mg for the meat itself. The table is where the sodium actually lives: cheddar runs 600–700 mg per 100 g and salted butter around 700 mg, so putting out a small dish of unsalted butter alongside the regular one is the single highest-value swap. A large sourdough roll is 400–500 mg on its own and is worth factoring in.

  8. Sunday, 12:30 — serve

    Split the rolls and put everything out in bowls so people build their own between turns. Lettuce leaf into the roll first as a barrier, then lamb, then sauce — that order keeps the bread from going soft over a long afternoon.

The spread

Everything below goes out cold, in bowls, at 12:15. Nothing here needs cooking or timing.

  • Sourdough rolls, split 400–500 mg sodium each
  • Yoghurt-mint sauce, homemade set some aside unsalted
  • Mint sauce, from the jar check the label — often high
  • Grated cheddar 600–700 mg per 100 g
  • Baby cos leaves negligible
  • Sliced cucumber negligible
  • Sliced tomato negligible
  • Butter, salted and unsalted put both out

Slow cooker capacity: 5.5 L or larger. Leftovers keep three days and are excellent in a wrap. Sodium figures are typical Australian supermarket values — check your own labels.
